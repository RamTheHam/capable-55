## Principles

- Treat the work as retraining, not as a generic beginner or age-norm program.
- Develop broad capability rather than optimising one sport.
- Train strength and aerobic capacity concurrently when recovery supports it.
- Train power explicitly, with low fatigue and suitable risk.
- Rebuild skill and force faster only when tissue tolerance and movement quality permit it.
- Use historical performance to understand potential, not to dictate today's load.
- Prefer a lower-dose version of the system to an all-or-nothing break.
- Treat sleep and recovery as performance variables, not moral scores.
- Use body composition as a trend and guardrail, not the main objective.
- Measure real-world capacity as well as gym and laboratory proxies.
- Let methods change while preserving observations, reasoning and history.
- Keep agency at the centre: “I own my life.” Imperfect weeks are expected; the system should make returning easy.
