# 2026 Q3 assessment and rebuild

- **Status:** Active
- **Phase label:** 2026-Q3
- **Exact block start/end dates:** `TBD`

## Aim

Establish a trustworthy current baseline and rebuild broad training tolerance after detraining. The evidence base discusses an initial 12-week retraining block, but its exact calendar dates and detailed prescriptions have not been supplied.

## What this phase must answer

- Which historical capacities return quickly, and which lag?
- What training dose fits the roughly five-hour weekly budget without an avoidable recovery cost?
- Which current movement restrictions or load-tolerance limits matter in practice?
- What mix of CrossFit, dedicated strength, aerobic work and real-world activity produces a sustainable trajectory?
- Does extending sleep and retaining the noon caffeine cutoff improve morning energy and training consistency?

## Confirmed design direction

These are boundaries supported by the imported evidence, not a complete weekly prescription:

- combine strength and aerobic work rather than isolate them into long single-quality phases;
- include low-fatigue power exposure;
- retain CrossFit for intensity, breadth, skill and enjoyment, while scaling early exposure;
- rebuild strict pulling, trunk control and controlled kipping before repeated muscle-up attempts;
- supply early aerobic volume through lower-impact modes while running tolerance returns;
- use submaximal strength work and avoid immediate tests of historical maxima;
- include a real-world low-to-moderate outing as a direct capacity check;
- adjust load from observed response, not historical identity.

## Working weekly template

The conversation established this first-pass structure. It is a template to scale within roughly five hours, not permission to invent loads or train through a poor response.

| Session | Working content | Nominal duration |
|---|---|---:|
| A | Strength plus gymnastics: squat/leg strength, press, strict pulling, trunk work and 10–15 minutes of hollow/arch, scapular or handstand skill | 70 min |
| B | Zone 2 by bike, rower, SkiErg or easy running once impact tolerance permits | 60 min |
| C | CrossFit/class, scaled before technique deteriorates | 60 min |
| D | Strength plus power: hinge, unilateral leg work, pull/press, carries and a low-fatigue jump or throw selected after assessment | 60–70 min |
| E | Engine/adventure through longer Zone 2, hiking, cycling, paddling and later running | 60–90 min |

The nominal sessions can exceed five hours. Shorten or rotate them so the actual week stays near the time budget and recovery remains acceptable.

## Twelve-week progression

| Weeks | Working emphasis |
|---|---|
| 1–4 | Re-entry. Most strength work around RPE 6–7 with 2–4 repetitions in reserve; technical gymnastics; aerobic work mainly easy. |
| 5–8 | Build. Add load and volume gradually; introduce explosive pulling, handstand work and controlled kipping only when shoulder capacity is suitable; add an occasional aerobic quality session. |
| 9–11 | Perform. Let training feel more athletic through heavier strength, power, harder engine work and more specific gymnastics without maximising several qualities at once. |
| 12 | Reduce volume, repeat the same standardised tests and review response, tolerance, bottlenecks and enjoyment before writing Block 2. |

## Monitoring

Every real session goes into the append-only training ledger defined in [`../training/README.md`](../training/README.md). Capture duration, work completed, session RPE when reported, symptoms or limiting body parts, and next-day response when available. Do not convert absence of a report into a zero.

## Decision gates

- Add load only when recent sessions show tolerable response and enough recovery.
- Progress impact or kipping only after the relevant prerequisite and tolerance data exist.
- Change the weekly mix when repeated data show poor recovery, poor adherence or a stalled priority outcome.
- Record any substantive change and its evidence in a dated review before rewriting the plan.
